needed-by: Agents, built 2026-11-11 (lane E; the follow-up the manager's review of #784 named, found by hostile tests of the manifest and `kai-memory 1`)
decision: A manifest's `net` destination is refused when its full written form is over `netdest`'s 1,024-byte limit, so every manifest that is read can be written and read again
---
`Manifest::format` writes the destination in `netdest`'s canonical form, which spells out `proto:tcp,addrs:none,` where a manifest may leave them out: up to 21 bytes longer than the text that was read. A destination within 21 bytes of `netdest::MAX_TEXT` (1,024) written short was read, then written longer than `Dest::parse` accepts, so a manifest that had been read could not be read back. The same hole was on main before the net budget. `net::parse` now refuses a destination whose canonical text is over the limit, with a refusal in the net line's words ("too long once written in full"); a test builds four 251-byte hosts that are 1,023 bytes short and 1,044 in full, refused, and three that read back.

The hostile tests that look for this are new: `kaiagent/tests/hostile.rs` mutates and truncates three manifests (the ADR's example, an interactive one with a net budget, one at the limits) with the shared test kit and holds every result to "read or refused, never a panic, bounded allocation, and what is read is written as a manifest that reads back as the same one, which writes itself", plus every line dropped, doubled or swapped refused. `kaimem/tests/hostile.rs` does the same for `kai-memory 1` (60,000 mutants and every prefix; ids below `next`, none twice, one line of at most 200 bytes), and found nothing: its decoder was already strict. `goalcore` and `semindex` already had such tests.

needed-by: ADR 0001 D5 and D6, built 2026-11-25 (lane K, bug hunt 2's H2-18 and H2-17; the manager reviewed #1364 and chose to keep ABI 1.5)
decision: A grant's revoke reaches everything made from a handle given under it: a `NetDest`'s narrowings and the `Stream`s connected on either, which are aborted at once. GRANT_DUP gives a handle that is under a grant node only under that node or one below it, and refuses any other with Permission, on ABI 1.5.
---
GRANT_REVOKE says every handle given under a revoked node answers Revoked from then on. Three paths broke that. The `NetDest` calls read their handle without its grant, so RESOLVE_ON, NETDEST_NARROW and NET_CONNECT still worked once it was revoked. NETDEST_NARROW gave its new handle no grant. GRANT_DUP could give a copy under a node outside the source's own, where a revoke of the source's node never reached it. No production path put a `NetDest` under a grant yet. ADR 0001 D6's run-tier handles for `scriptrun` will.

**What follows the grant.** A narrowing's handle carries the grant node of the handle it was made from. A `Stream`'s handle, and its record in `net::stream`, carry the grant node of the `NetDest` it was connected on. RESOLVE_ON, NETDEST_NARROW, NET_CONNECT, NET_SEND, NET_RECV and NET_POLL read the handle through its live grant, the same generation check the tree uses elsewhere, and answer Revoked once it is gone. A lookup or a NET_POLL under way when the node is revoked ends with Revoked. A connect that races the revoke is either refused or found by the abort.

**Open streams are aborted at the revoke.** The other choice was to let them fail at their next use. That would have left the connection up and let the send buffer drain to the peer after the person had taken the grant back. `handle::grant::revoke` calls `net::stream::on_revoke`, as it calls `shm::on_revoke` for shared pages. Each stream under a dead node is aborted, one poll sends its reset, and its socket and buffers go. Its place stays, with no socket, until its handle is closed, so its id never names a later stream. NET_CLOSE and HANDLE_CLOSE still close the handle. Such a place does not count against the stream limits.

**GRANT_DUP's new refusal.** An entry has one grant node. A copy given under a node outside the source's subtree cannot answer to both: revoking the new node must revoke it, and so must revoking the source's node. The copy now goes only under the source's node or one below it, where revoking the source's node revokes both. Any other node is refused with Permission. A handle with no node, a startup handle, still goes under any node. ADR 0001 D10 bumps the major for a changed meaning. This refusal only makes GRANT_REVOKE keep its written meaning, as audit K #17's new refusal on RESOLVE_ON did (`2026-11-19-netdest-private-answers`), so the ABI stays 1.5. The note beside the written exception in `kabi::handle::ABI` names it. No in-tree caller copied a handle that was under a node.

**H2-17, in the same change.** A refused HANDLE_REPLACE closes its handle either way, as the ABI says. On a `Stream` it left the socket open with no handle to reach it. The refusal now hands back the entry it closed (`Table::replace_raw`), and HANDLE_REPLACE lets it go as HANDLE_CLOSE does, which closes the stream.

**Tests.**
- Host tests in captable cover `replace_raw` and `Tree::may_give_under`.
- The boot case `net-stream` revokes a `NetDest`'s grant while `netcheck` holds streams on it and on its narrowing. Its streams are aborted at once, and every use after answers Revoked. In the same case, 17 streams are each refused a HANDLE_REPLACE, and none reaches the limit.
- The boot case `handles` checks GRANT_DUP under a node beside the source's (refused) and below it (given, and revoked with that node).

needed-by: P5 task 2, fixed 2026-11-25 (bug hunt #13; 32 slots or 64 with a smaller buffer is the founder's choice, still open)
decision: A closed TCP stream counts against every stream limit until its close is done, and is aborted `kabi::net::STREAM_CLOSE_MS` (5 s) after its close if it is not done by then; `STREAMS_MAX` goes from 64 to 32, so every slot full takes under half the kernel heap
---
**The fault.** NET_CLOSE moved a stream to a closing list that no limit counted, and the list held up to 32 before it aborted the oldest. A closed stream keeps its socket, and so its buffers, until its close is done, so one process could open its 16, close them, and open 16 more, again and again: 64 open slots were 23 MiB of buffers, and 32 closing bulk streams 34 MiB more, past the 32 MiB heap. A peer that never sent its FIN kept a stream closing for ever: smoltcp's own timeout counts from the peer's last segment, so a peer that keeps answering is never timed out.

**The fix.** The limits (16 a process, `STREAMS_MAX` in all, 4 bulk in all and 2 a process) count open and closing streams together (`netcore::streams::Limits::allow`, tested on the host). Each closed stream has a deadline 5 s after its close, over FIN-WAIT, CLOSING and LAST-ACK alike; past it the stream is aborted, its reset goes out on the next poll, and it is removed (`netcore::streams::linger`). A connect first sweeps the closes done since the last poll, and reserves room for every stream to be closing, so a close never allocates. A compile-time check keeps the worst case at or under half the heap: 32 x (256 KiB + 64 KiB) = 10 MiB, and 4 bulk streams 768 KiB more each = 3 MiB, 13 MiB in all (`Limits::worst_case`). A process that closes and reconnects at once may now get Busy for up to 5 s while its closes finish; `netcheck`'s stream mode waits that out, and `test=net-stream` fails if a closed stream is still closing past the deadline and a second for its reset.

**Open: 32 or 64.** Halving `STREAMS_MAX` is the default in this fix, not a decision. The other way keeps 64 slots and drops the receive buffer from 256 KiB to 128 KiB: 64 x (128 KiB + 64 KiB) = 12 MiB, and 4 bulk streams 896 KiB more each = 3.5 MiB, 15.5 MiB in all, also under half the heap. That keeps twice the streams, but a plain stream's window halves, and with it the most one stream can receive at a given round trip (about 13 MB/s at 20 ms with 256 KiB, about 6.5 MB/s with 128 KiB); bulk streams keep their 1 MiB either way. The founder chooses; the pull request is not labelled for a batch until then.

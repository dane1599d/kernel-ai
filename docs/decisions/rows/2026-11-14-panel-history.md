decision: The panel's lines and replies show as a chat on the desk above the bar; the model's words show there in the AI's own look, never in the bar
---
The founder's first hands-on test of the desktop (VirtualBox, `cargo xtask vbox`) found that asking a question in the bar looked like nothing happened: the bar showed "answered in words: ask in the terminal" and never the answer. They asked for the panel's requests and replies to "show up in the background like how chat gpt and claude are". That is the product's OK to draw the model's words on the desk, which ADR 0005 §8 and P3's trusted-layer rule kept out of the panel. The safeguard holds: the bar's state line is still code's alone ("Kai answered above." for words), and the model's words and a model script's prints go only to the history. The shell sends them to `desktop` as `panel said <text>` (`deskcore::panel_wire`), before the line's state; the history (`deskcore::chat`) also keeps the person's line and the bar's result line or refusal as a note. `desktop` draws it on the desk's background, under every window, in a centred column at most 760 px wide. The AI's words get a `Kai` label in `ai`, a 2 px `ai` rule down their left and sans `text`; the person's lines and the notes are mono, as the system's own words are, so the model's text cannot pass for the system's. A line that asks first, or that leaves a question open, is still the terminal's: no script is shown in the history, and its note says to use the terminal. The history keeps 40 entries of at most 4,000 bytes each, drops control characters, takes no input, and is drawn before the windows, the bar and any card, so it can cover none of them. ADR 0005 §8 has a paragraph saying so. Follow-ups: a control in the note that opens the terminal for a line that asks first, and scrolling back through older entries.

decision: A send typed at the desk is answered on the trusted card, which shows the send row's code-made call (where it goes and the text's SHA-256 prefix); the text shows in the Kai pane's history rectangle in the AI look under a code header (bytes, where, how far read), drawn only when its own hash matches the card's; Allow opens only once the whole text has been on screen
---
Until now a send (`net_send`, up to 4 KB of text) typed at the desk was refused there and asked only on the console. Now the card shows the send row's call as the console's card does (#989), `net_send("host", "/path", "<16 hex>")`, the last argument the first 16 hex digits of the text's SHA-256; the `ONCE` names that call, and the shell sends only a text with that digest. `desktop` shows the text in the Kai pane's history rectangle, in the AI look, never in the card or the trusted look, under a header code writes: `<n> bytes to <host><path> · SHA-256 <16 hex> · lines a-b of c`. It draws the text only when its own SHA-256 of the bytes begins with the digest in the kernel's posted call. Allow opens only once the whole text has been on screen: it fits, or the person scrolled to its end with the wheel or PageUp/PageDown, which `desktop`'s code routes while the card is up. The desk offers the card only where the card and the open pane fit side by side with the history rectangle uncovered, decided from geometry; elsewhere (today 1280x800 and 1024x768) the send is asked at the console. A send's card that comes up with the pane closed opens the pane first, and takes no Allow until the text is drawn there and read through. After Allow the send is held 5 s with undo. Agents' sends are not part of this. Design: lane C's note of 2026-11-18, with the manager's review rulings of 2026-11-19. The amendment is ADR 0005 §7, "Amended 2026-11-19".

needed-by: Agents, built 2026-11-09 (lane E; the manager asked for a net budget in `kaiagent` and an end-to-end test; host crates only, no OS wiring before the tag)
decision: A manifest's `net` line may say the bytes a day its network may move (`bytes/day=Nm`, 10 MiB by default, 512 MiB at most), counted by a pure `DayBytes` counter
---
ADR 0013 section 6 bounds an agent's tokens, KV positions and pool share (the `model` line) but not what its network may move, and an agent that can reach a host could move as much as the link allows. The `net` line now takes a budget after the destination: `net proto:tcp,ports:443,hosts:api.weather.example bytes/day=10m`.

**The two numbers are estimates, not founder-approved.** ADR 0013 has no network byte budget: question 10's approved defaults are the tokens a day, the KV positions and the pool share, and `net` stays `none` until P5. The 10 MiB default and the 512 MiB ceiling go to the founder, with P5's questions, before the network service charges this counter. Nothing in the OS reads them now.

- **Grammar.** `bytes/day=Nm`, N whole MiB above zero and at most 512, in the shape of `pool=64m`. It is refused the same ways (`BudgetNumber`, `BudgetZero`, `BudgetOver`, with the key `net bytes/day`) and in the net line's own words for the key and the unit (`NetBudgetKey`, `NetBudgetUnit`). The destination is judged first, so a bad destination is refused as it always was whatever follows it. A destination that says nothing takes the default, 10 MiB (est.: a few hundred fetches of an API answer or a page); `net none` moves nothing and takes no budget (`none bytes/day=1m` is refused). `Manifest` gains `net_budget` (`net` is unchanged), and `Manifest::format` always writes it after a destination, so what is written reads back as the same manifest (one hole, the same on main: a destination near `netdest`'s text limit that formats longer than it was written is refused on re-read; a follow-up).
- **The ceiling, 512 MiB a day (est.),** is well past what an agent that reads pages and calls APIs needs. A request for more is a download and not a budget, and P5's verified downloads are for that.
- **The counter.** `kaiagent::DayBytes` is pure: no clock, the caller says the day (the host's calendar day, as `agentsched`'s `Now.day`). A later day starts again at zero; an earlier day counts as the last one, so a date set back gives nothing back. Going forward is trusted: each step forward pays a fresh budget, and a clock set ahead starts one at once with no reset when the calendar catches up, since the counter cannot tell a real midnight from a set clock. A charge that would pass the limit is refused whole and counts nothing (a transfer stops at the byte it cannot pay for), **a charge of zero is always paid**, even over a limit that was lowered below what was used, an overflowing sum is refused and never wraps, and the limit can change with what was used staying. `DayBytes` is not `Copy`, so a charge on a copy cannot be lost. The network service will charge it after the tag.
- **The end-to-end test** (`agentid/tests/end_to_end.rs`, with `agentsched` and `goalcore` as dev-dependencies) is one flow with fakes, and it shows what each crate does on its own side: a manifest becomes an agent with a badge; its grants are the door's ceiling (`fs_trash` is `OutsideGrants`); the person's words go to `kai` and not to it; the scheduler runs its planning requests under its 600 tokens a day with the person's request first and refuses the next one over quota until the day turns; each of its goal's steps is admitted and stamped `agent=report-bot`; a step outside the goal's folders asks, the agent's own answer is refused (`NotThePerson`), a yes for that call cannot be spent on another call of the same agent, and the person's yes runs it. The goal's steps are joined to the agent by the test's own order and entry numbers: `goalcore` never sees the roster, so the OS wiring after the tag is what joins them for real.

No WIT, no package kind, no `actlog` and no OS crate changes; `kaiagent`, `agentid` and their dev-dependencies are host crates.

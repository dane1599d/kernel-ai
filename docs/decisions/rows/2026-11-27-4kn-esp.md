needed-by: P7 (real boards), P8 (installer), built 2026-11-27 (row G58 B; S-io)
decision: On a 4Kn disk the GPT is in 4 KiB LBAs, but the ESP and slot volumes keep 512-byte FAT32 BPBs, byte for byte the same as on a 512-byte disk; proven under OVMF only
---
G58 boots from disks of 4 KiB logical blocks. The GPT has to follow the disk: on a 4Kn disk the header is at byte 4096, the entry array is 4 LBAs, and partitions are counted in 4 KiB LBAs (`diskmap::layout_in`, `xtask/src/image/gpt.rs` `layout_on`). The FAT32 volumes inside the partitions are the open question. A FAT BPB names its own bytes per sector, and there are two ways to set it on 4Kn media.

**1. Keep 512-byte BPBs (chosen).** The ESP and kai-r, kai-a and kai-b keep the volumes they have on a 512-byte disk. The hidden-sector count is still the partition's start in 512-byte units. The partitions sit at the same bytes, so the whole disk past the GPT is byte for byte the same.
- `slot.img`, the signed update target, is one image for both kinds of disk. `updated` writes it into a slot as it always has. Its `image_sectors` reads the BPB's 512-byte sector count, which stays right.
- The installer copies the ESP and the committed slot byte for byte (row `2026-11-27-installer-plan`). A 4Kn target would get the same volumes, and only the GPT would differ. The installer is 512-only today and refuses a 4Kn target; that is a later change to `installer::plan`, not a reformat.
- `PARTITION_MIB` (64, 256, 1024, 1024) is unchanged.
- The risk: the UEFI spec does not promise that firmware mounts a FAT whose BPB sector size differs from the media's block size. EDK2's FAT driver (OVMF) reads through DiskIo at byte granularity and accepts it, and Limine reads by byte offset using the BPB. Some vendor firmware and Linux's vfat check the two against each other, and Linux refuses such a volume. Linux mounting the ESP is not on our boot path; firmware is.

**2. A 4096-byte BPB and a 260 MiB or larger ESP.** This is the common choice for 4Kn, and Microsoft's minimum. FAT32 needs at least 65,525 clusters, and with 4 KiB sectors that is about 256 MiB.
- It breaks `PARTITION_MIB`. The 64 MiB ESP cannot be FAT32 with 4 KiB sectors, and kai-r at 256 MiB sits just under the floor once its reserved sectors and FATs are counted.
- Each slot volume and `slot.img` would need a 4096-byte variant, so a release would carry two signed slot images and `updated` would pick one by the disk's block size.
- The installer could not copy the ESP or a slot byte for byte between disks of different block sizes. It would have to reformat and copy files, which is what espfat deliberately does not do (decision row `2026-11-26-espfat-own-fat32`).

Option 1 is the smaller change, keeps one signed image, and is what OVMF boots. Option 2 is what real 4Kn boards are known to accept. We take option 1 now, prove it in CI, and revisit it at P7 if a target board refuses it.

**Proof.** The boot cases `slot-4k` (virtio-blk) and `slot-4k-nvme` (NVMe) boot a slot disk with `logical_block_size=4096`: R installs and arms A, then A boots and commits. They run under OVMF in CI only, on KVM for PRs and TCG for batches.

Part C (G58 C) puts the whole system on 4Kn disks. `os-4k` and `os-4k-nvme` carry `kai-data` as partition 5 of the boot disk, with the model on a 4 KiB disk of its own: A writes files through `fs`, and the next boot reads them back. `update-4k` (from the update disk) and `stage-4k-nvme` (a copy of the running slot) have `updated` write slot B, which then boots. A slot image's length is a whole number of 512-byte sectors, not of 4 KiB blocks, and a 4Kn disk refuses a write of part of a block. So `updated` pads every SLOT_WRITE to whole `kabi::update::BLOCK`s (4 KiB), zeros past the image's end, and starts each write on a block. That stays inside the slot's partition, which is whole MiB. The signed image is unchanged.

**Risk for P7.** Each real board must boot a 4Kn disk with option 1 before it is a supported target. Most consumer NVMe drives ship formatted 512e, which is not affected. A drive reformatted to 4Kn (`nvme format --lbaf`) is the case to test on each board's firmware. If a board refuses it, the fix is option 2's layout for 4Kn disks only, with its second slot image. That is a format change, and it would get its own row.
